Your website doesn't rank because Google can't figure out what your pages are about, who they're for, or why it should trust you over a competitor. That sounds blunt, but in nearly every Nepali business site I've audited, the cause comes down to the same short list: pages with almost no useful content, no clear search terms being targeted, no local business signals, and a site that loads too slowly on the phones most Nepalis actually browse with.

**Who this is for:** Nepali business owners whose website gets little or no traffic from Google, and who want an honest diagnosis before spending money.

**Who this is NOT for:** Sites that already get steady search traffic and want advanced growth tactics — that's a different playbook, and this guide won't cover it.

## TL;DR

- Most Nepali business websites fail at SEO for the same handful of reasons: the site was designed but never optimized, pages have thin or no content, nothing targets real search terms, local signals are missing, and the site is slow on mobile.
- The fixes are not mysterious. They are unglamorous work: useful content, clear page structure, a claimed Google Business Profile, and technical cleanup.
- Do the fixes in the order listed below — each one builds on the last.
- If you want an honest assessment of where your site stands, [talk to an SEO specialist](/seo-expert-in-nepal) who measures before recommending.

## The 7 reasons your site doesn't rank

### 1. The site was built to look good, not to be found

This is the single most common cause. A designer or agency built a beautiful site, launched it, and nobody ever asked "what should this page rank for?" The homepage is a slideshow and a tagline. Service pages are a paragraph and a contact form. There is no keyword plan, no page titles written for search, and often no sitemap submitted to Google Search Console.

A website without an SEO plan is a shop with no signboard. It exists, but nobody walking past knows what's inside. The fix starts before design: decide which searches each page should capture, then build the page around that intent. If your site is already live, a [technical SEO audit](/blog/technical-seo-audit-checklist-nepal) finds exactly what's missing.

### 2. Pages have almost nothing useful to say

Google ranks pages that answer questions well. Many Nepali business sites have service pages of 100–200 words that say "we provide quality service at affordable prices" and nothing else. That text answers no question, solves no problem, and gives Google no reason to rank the page.

**The rule of thumb:** if your page doesn't say more than a competitor's page — and say it more clearly — it won't outrank them. Write pages that answer what a customer actually asks before buying: what you do, how it works, what it costs (or what affects cost), how long it takes, and why they should pick you. This is the core of [on-page SEO](/blog/on-page-seo-basics-nepali-businesses), and it's the highest-leverage work most small sites can do.

### 3. Nothing targets what people actually search

A lot of sites use the words the *business* uses internally, not the words customers type into Google. A clinic writes "holistic wellness services" when patients search "skin doctor in Kathmandu." A hotel writes "boutique accommodation experience" when travelers search "hotel in Thamel."

Matching real search language is half the battle, especially in Nepal where people search in a mix of English, Romanized Nepali, and Nepali script. When I grew a client ecommerce site from near-zero visibility to 265K Google Search impressions and 6.76K clicks in 8 months (average position 8.2), the unglamorous foundation was this: every important page targeted a specific search phrase real customers use, and the content answered the intent behind it.

### 4. Local signals are missing

For any business serving customers in a place — Kathmandu, Pokhara, Chitwan, anywhere — local signals are how Google decides you're a real business worth showing. The biggest gaps I see:

- **No claimed Google Business Profile**, or one with the wrong category, no photos, and three reviews from 2019.
- **Inconsistent business details** — the name, address, or phone number differs between the website, Facebook page, and directory listings.
- **No location pages** — a restaurant in Jhamsikhel with one generic "our menu" page gives Google nothing to connect it to local searches like "restaurant in Jhamsikhel."

Local SEO is often the fastest win for Nepali businesses. A claimed, complete [Google Business Profile plus Maps optimization](/blog/local-seo-kathmandu-google-business-profile) can start showing results in weeks, not months, because most local competitors haven't done it either.

### 5. The site is slow on the phones people actually use

Most Nepali internet users browse on mid-range Android phones over mobile data. A site that loads in 2 seconds on a developer's laptop in Kathmandu can take 8+ seconds on a real user's phone — and Google measures that. Slow pages rank worse, and visitors who do arrive leave before the page finishes loading.

Common culprits: oversized images uploaded straight from a camera, too many plugins on WordPress sites, cheap shared hosting, and heavy page builders. The fixes — compressing images, trimming plugins, caching — are usually a day or two of developer work, not a rebuild.

### 6. Nobody else on the internet mentions you

Google treats links and mentions from other sites as votes of confidence. Most small Nepali business sites have almost none: no local news coverage, no directory listings beyond Facebook, no supplier or partner pages linking back. Without that external trust, even good content struggles.

The honest version of link building is simple: be worth mentioning. Publish genuinely useful content, get listed in real directories, build relationships with local press and bloggers, and earn links the slow way. Anyone promising you "100 backlinks for Rs. 5,000" is selling the kind of spammy links that get sites penalized, not ranked. I never build those, for any client, at any price.

### 7. Technical blockers stop Google from reading the site at all

Sometimes the problem is mechanical: the site isn't indexed. I've seen Nepali sites with a `noindex` tag left on from development, robots.txt files blocking everything, no XML sitemap, or JavaScript-heavy pages Google can't render properly. The site owner assumes SEO "isn't working" when Google has literally never been invited in.

This is a ten-minute check with Google Search Console: is the site indexed, are there crawl errors, is the sitemap submitted? If you're a developer yourself, fixing these is trivial. If not, it's exactly the kind of thing an SEO person with development skills handles in the first week. (I'm both — an [SEO specialist and full-stack developer](/seo-expert-in-nepal) — so technical blockers never need a second contractor.)

## What to fix first: the priority order

| Priority | Problem | Symptom you'll notice | DIY or get help? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Not indexed / technical blockers | Site doesn't appear even when you search its exact name | DIY with Search Console, or 1–2 hours of a developer |
| 2 | Slow mobile experience | High bounce rate, poor mobile scores | Developer, usually 1–2 days |
| 3 | No Google Business Profile | Competitors show on Maps, you don't | DIY, free |
| 4 | Thin, unfocused content | Pages exist but rank for nothing | Biggest effort — write or hire a writer |
| 5 | Wrong keywords / no keyword plan | Traffic comes, but from irrelevant searches | SEO help or careful research |
| 6 | No local signals | Ranking in general but invisible locally | Mostly DIY |
| 7 | No external trust / links | Good content that still doesn't move | Slow, ongoing work |

Work down this table in order. Fixing content before fixing indexing is like painting a house with no foundation — do the foundation first.

## Honest tradeoffs you should know

- **SEO is slow.** Even with everything fixed, expect 3–6 months before meaningful traffic for a new or neglected site, longer in competitive niches. Anyone promising page one in 30 days is lying or cheating. [Realistic timelines](/blog/how-long-does-seo-take-nepal) matter more than enthusiasm.
- **Content takes real effort.** There's no shortcut around writing pages that genuinely help your customers. AI can draft, but someone who knows the business has to shape it.
- **Some fixes need a developer.** If your site is on a locked-down builder or a theme nobody can edit, technical fixes may require rebuilding — and that's sometimes the right call.
- **Ads are faster.** If you need customers this month, not this year, [Facebook and Instagram ads](/blog/seo-vs-facebook-ads-nepal) will beat SEO on speed. The smart play is often both: ads now, SEO building underneath.

## FAQs

**My website looks great but gets no visitors from Google. Why?**
Because looking good and ranking are different jobs. Ranking requires Google to find your pages, understand what they're about, trust your business, and decide your page answers the search better than alternatives. A beautiful site with thin content, no keyword targeting, and no local signals fails most of those checks. Design is the paint; SEO is the engine.

**How do I check if Google even knows my site exists?**
Search Google for `site:yourdomain.com`. If no pages show up, your site isn't indexed — check Search Console for why. If pages show up but rank poorly, the problem is content, keywords, or trust, not visibility.

**Should my site be in English or Nepali?**
Follow your customers. Many Nepali searches happen in English, many in Romanized Nepali ("skin doctor kathmandu"), and a growing share in Nepali script. Check what people actually type — Google Search Console and the autocomplete suggestions are free research tools. Don't guess; look at the data.

**My competitor ranks higher with an uglier website. How?**
Because Google doesn't judge design. It judges relevance, content depth, site speed, and trust signals. An ugly site with 2,000 useful words, fast loading, and real reviews will beat a beautiful site with 150 words every time.

**How long before these fixes show results?**
Technical fixes and a claimed Business Profile can show movement in weeks. Content and keyword improvements typically take 2–4 months to mature. Competitive keywords can take 6+ months. The timeline depends on your niche and how far behind you're starting — [here's the full breakdown](/blog/how-long-does-seo-take-nepal).

**Should I rebuild my website or fix the current one?**
Fix first, rebuild only if necessary. Most ranking problems are content and technical issues, not design problems. Rebuild only when the platform itself blocks fixes — uneditable themes, no access to the code, or a site so slow that optimization costs more than starting over. And if you do rebuild, [choose the right SEO help](/blog/how-to-choose-seo-expert-in-nepal) before the first mockup, not after launch.

## Final recommendation

If your Nepali business website gets no search traffic, don't start with a redesign and don't buy a "guaranteed ranking" package. Start with the checklist in priority order: confirm you're indexed, fix mobile speed, claim your Google Business Profile, then do the unglamorous work of writing pages that answer real customer questions with the words they actually search.

Most Nepali business websites don't rank for fixable, ordinary reasons — not because SEO is magic and not because Google is unfair.
