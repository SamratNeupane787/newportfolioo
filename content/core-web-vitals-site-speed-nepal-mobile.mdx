## What Are Core Web Vitals, in Plain Language?

Core Web Vitals are three measurements Google uses to judge whether a page feels good to use. They come from real visitors' browsers, not from a lab test, which is why they matter: they describe what your actual customers experience.

| Metric | What it measures | What "good" looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Largest Contentful Paint (LCP)** | How long until the biggest visible thing on the page — usually the main image or headline — appears | Under 2.5 seconds |
| **Interaction to Next Paint (INP)** | How quickly the page responds when someone taps a button, link, or menu | Under 200 milliseconds |
| **Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS)** | How much the page jumps around while loading — text moving, buttons shifting under a thumb | A score under 0.1 |

Think of it this way. **LCP** is "how long am I staring at a blank screen?" **INP** is "when I tap, does anything happen?" **CLS** is "did the button move just as I was about to tap it, so I hit the wrong thing?" Every Nepali mobile user has experienced all three failures, usually on the same site.

Google folds these measurements into its ranking systems. They are not the biggest ranking factor — relevance and content still matter more — but when two pages offer similar content, the faster one wins. And long before rankings, speed decides whether a visitor stays at all: a page that loads in 2 seconds keeps far more visitors than one that loads in 6, on any connection.

You can check your own scores free in Google's PageSpeed Insights tool — run it on your homepage and your top service pages, and look at the **mobile** results, not desktop. That mobile score is the one your customers live with.

## Why Speed Matters More in Nepal

In markets with fast, stable connections, a heavy page is an annoyance. In Nepal, it is a deal-breaker, for three reasons.

**First, mobile is not a segment — it is the market.** Most Nepali customers discover businesses on their phones, through Google search, Facebook, and WhatsApp links. A site designed and tested on a desktop, then never checked on a real phone, is a site built for the wrong audience.

**Second, connections are variable.** The same visitor may have fast Wi-Fi at home, a shaky 4G signal on the road, and a weak signal indoors. A page that loads in 3 seconds on Wi-Fi can take 10 seconds on a weak mobile signal. Your site has to work in the worst case, not the best one.

**Third, devices are modest.** Many Nepali users browse on budget Android phones with limited memory and processing power. A page with ten tracking scripts and a heavy animation may run fine on a developer's laptop and crawl on the phone your customer actually owns. Testing on a flagship phone tells you nothing about your real audience.

The practical consequence: a Nepali business that fixes speed gets a bigger advantage than the same fix would give in a fast-connection market, because competitors are slower here too. Most local business sites have never been speed-tested at all.

## What Actually Slows Nepali Websites Down

When I audit Nepali business sites, the same problems appear again and again. In rough order of impact:

### 1. Uncompressed, oversized images

This is the number one culprit. A photo straight from a phone camera can be 4–6 MB. Put five of those on a homepage and the page is 25 MB before a single word of text loads. Visitors on mobile data are being asked to download a small movie just to see your menu.

**The fix:** Resize images to the size they actually display (a full-width banner rarely needs more than 1600 pixels wide; a thumbnail needs a few hundred), compress them before uploading, and use modern formats like WebP where your site supports them. This one fix alone often cuts page weight by half or more.

### 2. Heavy themes, page builders, and plugins

Many Nepali sites were built with a feature-packed theme or page builder that loads styling and scripts for every feature on every page — sliders, animations, popup builders, social feeds — whether the page uses them or not. Each plugin adds its own scripts. Ten plugins can mean dozens of extra files per page load.

**The fix:** Audit what is actually installed. Remove plugins you do not use. If a slider is the only reason a heavy plugin exists, consider whether a single static image would do the same job faster. Simpler pages are faster pages.

### 3. Too many third-party scripts

Chat widgets, Facebook pixels, analytics tools, booking widgets, popups, ad scripts — each one is another download, another connection, and another chance to slow the page. Individually they seem small. Together they routinely add seconds.

**The fix:** List every script on your key pages and ask what each one earns. Keep analytics and anything that directly brings bookings. Question everything else. If a chat widget gets no chats, it is pure weight.

### 4. No caching, slow hosting

Without caching, every visitor's browser re-downloads the same files on every visit. Without decent hosting, even a light page waits on a slow server. Cheap shared hosting that puts hundreds of sites on one server is a common hidden cause of slow Nepali sites — the page itself is fine, but the server takes seconds to answer.

**The fix:** Turn on caching (your developer or host can do this in minutes) and, if your server response time is consistently over a second, consider better hosting before anything else. No amount of image compression fixes a server that answers slowly.

### 5. No lazy loading, no prioritization

Many sites load every image on the page immediately — including images far below the fold that the visitor may never scroll to. The browser is busy downloading the footer gallery while the headline is still blank.

**The fix:** Lazy-load images below the fold so they download only when the visitor scrolls near them. Load the content people see first, first.

## The Fix Order: What to Do First

Do not try to fix everything at once. Work in this order, because it captures the biggest wins earliest:

1. **Compress and resize images** on your homepage and top pages. Biggest single win, no technical skill required.
2. **Remove unused plugins and scripts.** A 30-minute audit with your developer.
3. **Turn on caching** and check your hosting response time. If the server is slow, fix or move hosting before optimizing further.
4. **Lazy-load below-the-fold images** and defer non-essential scripts.
5. **Re-test in PageSpeed Insights** on mobile, and fix whatever is now the biggest remaining problem — repeat until your Core Web Vitals pass.

Most small Nepali business sites can get from "slow" to "acceptable" with steps 1–3 alone, in a single afternoon of work. Getting from "acceptable" to "excellent" takes a developer and more time — and for most businesses, that second step is optional. The first step is not.

## Honest Tradeoffs

- **Speed helps rankings, but it is not a ranking strategy on its own.** A fast page with thin content still loses to a slower page that answers the search properly. Speed multiplies good content; it does not replace it. Pair this work with [on-page SEO basics](/blog/on-page-seo-basics-nepali-businesses) and a [blogging strategy](/blog/content-that-ranks-blogging-strategy-nepal) that gives people something worth loading.
- **Some speed work costs money.** Better hosting, a developer's time, a rebuilt theme — these are real costs. Weigh them against what slow pages cost you: every visitor who bounces is a customer you already paid to attract, through search, ads, or word of mouth.
- **Scores are a guide, not a grade.** A perfect 100 in PageSpeed Insights is not the goal. Passing Core Web Vitals on mobile for your most important pages is. Do not rebuild a working site chasing a perfect score.
- **Speed gains take weeks to show in rankings.** Google measures real visitors over time, so improvements appear gradually as new visitor data accumulates. Conversions, though, improve immediately — visitors who stay are visitors who can buy.

## FAQs

### What are Core Web Vitals in simple terms?

Core Web Vitals are Google's three measurements of how a page feels to a real visitor: how fast the main content appears (LCP), how quickly the page responds to taps and clicks (INP), and how much the layout shifts while loading (CLS). Together they describe whether using your site feels smooth or frustrating. Google uses them as a ranking signal, and they are measured from real users' browsers on your actual site.

### How fast should my website load in Nepal?

Aim for your main content to appear within 2.5 seconds on a mobile connection — that is Google's "good" threshold for LCP. In practice, many Nepali business sites currently take 6–10 seconds on mobile, so even getting under 4 seconds puts you ahead of most local competitors. Test on a real phone on mobile data, not just on office Wi-Fi, because that is the experience most of your customers have.

### Do Core Web Vitals actually affect my Google rankings?

Yes, but they are one signal among many. Google has confirmed that page experience, including Core Web Vitals, influences rankings — most noticeably when pages are otherwise similar. A fast site will not outrank a much better page, but between two similar pages, the faster one wins. The bigger effect is usually on conversions: faster pages keep more visitors, and kept visitors buy, book, and call.

### What is the most common reason Nepali websites are slow?

Uncompressed images, by a wide margin. Photos uploaded straight from a camera or phone, at full resolution, with no compression, routinely make up most of a page's weight. Before touching anything technical, compress and resize the images on your homepage and top pages — it is the cheapest, fastest, highest-impact fix available, and it needs no coding.

### Should I use a speed plugin or hire a developer?

For a small site, start with the no-code fixes: compress images, remove unused plugins, turn on caching. A caching or optimization plugin can help with the last two. Hire a developer when the site is large, when the theme itself is the problem, or when you have done the basics and scores are still failing. That is also the point where working with an [SEO expert in Nepal](/seo-expert-in-nepal) pays off: prioritizing the remaining fixes from real data instead of guessing.

### How do I check my Core Web Vitals for free?

Use Google's PageSpeed Insights: enter your URL, and it shows mobile and desktop scores plus the three Core Web Vitals. For ongoing data from real visitors, Google Search Console's Core Web Vitals report shows which of your pages pass or fail over time. Check both: PageSpeed Insights diagnoses a single page today, Search Console shows the trend across your whole site.

### Will fixing speed help if my content is weak?

Only partly. Speed keeps visitors on the page; content gives them a reason to stay and to convert. If your pages are thin — a few generic sentences and a contact button — fix the content first or alongside speed, using the [on-page checklist](/blog/on-page-seo-basics-nepali-businesses). The best results come from doing both: pages worth loading, that load fast.

### How long does it take for speed improvements to show in rankings?

Conversions improve immediately, because visitors stop bouncing. Rankings improve gradually over a few weeks, as Google collects new real-user data showing the improved experience. That timeline is consistent with [how long SEO generally takes for Nepali businesses](/blog/how-long-does-seo-take-nepal) — speed is one of the faster-moving pieces, but it is still measured over weeks, not days.
