Your SEO contract is coming up for renewal. The agency sends a report — a polished PDF, green arrows everywhere, impressions up, rankings up, traffic up. Everything looks good. So you sign again.

Six months later, the phone still is not ringing any more than before.

That gap between "the report looks great" and "the business feels no different" is the most common problem in SEO, and it is almost never a problem with Google. It is a problem with what the report measures, what it hides, and what nobody asked before the renewal was signed.

This guide is for business owners in Nepal who are staring at an SEO report and a renewal invoice, wondering whether the money did anything. It shows you exactly what to check, in what order, and which findings mean you should renegotiate — or walk away. If you want the full background on which metrics matter in the first place, read the companion guide on [SEO reporting metrics that matter](/blog/seo-reporting-metrics-that-matter-nepal).

**TL;DR**
- A good SEO report proves business results: more clicks from people searching for what you sell, more calls and enquiries from those clicks, and rankings for terms your customers actually use.
- Impressions, total traffic, and keyword counts are easy to inflate and prove almost nothing on their own.
- If all the growth is branded searches (people googling your own business name), the agency may have done nothing — those visitors were coming anyway.
- Demand a list of actual work: pages published, technical fixes shipped, and links earned with real URLs. Vague claims like "optimization done" are not deliverables.
- Check who owns the content, the accounts, and the data before you renew — and what happens to your rankings if you stop.

## Who this guide is for

This guide is for a business owner in Nepal who hired an SEO agency or freelancer, received monthly reports, and now faces a renewal decision. You do not need to know SEO yourself — you need a way to judge whether the money did anything, using the report in front of you.

## Who this guide is NOT for

It is not for judging a one-month-old campaign, where there is barely any data to check. It is not for businesses that never agreed on target keywords or business goals at the start — without those, no report can be judged fairly, and the fix is a better contract, not a better report. And it is not a guide to doing SEO yourself; it is a guide to deciding whether to keep paying someone else to do it.

## Start with the question the report should answer

An SEO report exists to answer one question: did the money turn into customers? Every number in it should connect back to that. When you open the report, keep that question in front of you and notice how quickly the numbers drift away from it. That drift is where weak agencies hide.

Most reports are built to impress, not to inform. They lead with the biggest numbers available — impressions, total sessions, total keywords — because big numbers feel like progress. Your job is to work past the headline numbers to the ones that describe real demand for your business.

## Check 1: Clicks from search, not impressions

Impressions count how many times your pages appeared in search results. Clicks count how many times someone actually visited. Impressions can double while clicks stay flat — that usually means you are ranking on page two or three for more terms, where almost nobody clicks, or ranking for terms nobody wants to click.

What to check:

- **Organic clicks over time, from Google Search Console.** Ask for a chart covering the whole contract period, not just the last 30 days. A real trend rises gradually with ups and downs. A flat line with a good final month is cherry-picking.
- **Clicks versus impressions.** If impressions grew 200% and clicks grew 10%, the new visibility is not turning into visits. Ask why.
- **Which pages earned the clicks.** A handful of blog posts bringing informational traffic is fine, but if your service pages — the ones that sell — earned nothing, the SEO is not reaching buyers.

A real example from my own work: a Nepali ecommerce site went from near-zero to 265,000 impressions and 6,760 clicks in 8 months, at an average position of 8.2. The impressions number is the one that sounds impressive. The clicks number is the one that mattered, because those were visits to product and category pages. ## Check 2: Branded versus non-branded searches

This is the single most revealing check, and most reports never show it.

A branded search is someone googling your business name: "Hotel Himalaya Kathmandu," "your clinic name + reviews." Those people already knew you. They were coming to your site with or without SEO. A non-branded search is someone googling what you sell: "hotel in Thamel with parking," "dentist in Lalitpur," "trekking company Nepal." Those are new customers, and winning them is the entire point of hiring an SEO agency.

What to check:

- **Ask for clicks split into branded and non-branded.** In Search Console, this means filtering queries that contain your business name versus queries that do not. Any competent agency can produce this in five minutes.
- **If non-branded clicks are flat while total clicks grew,** the growth came from your own brand getting more famous — through word of mouth, ads, or social media — not from SEO.
- **If the agency cannot or will not split the data,** that is itself an answer. It usually means the split looks bad.

This check alone has ended more renewals than any other. Run it first.

## Check 3: Rankings for terms that bring customers

Ranking reports are the easiest thing in SEO to manipulate. An agency can show you 200 keywords on page one — if 190 of them are your own business name, misspellings of it, or phrases so specific that nobody searches them, those rankings are worthless.

What to check:

- **Your money terms.** Before the contract started, you should have agreed on a list of search terms that describe what you sell and where you sell it. Check positions for those specific terms, not for the agency's chosen list.
- **Terms with real search demand.** A number-one ranking for a phrase nobody types is not a win. Ask which of the reported keywords get meaningful monthly searches in Nepal.
- **Movement on competitive terms.** Going from position 48 to position 31 is movement, but it brings zero clicks. What matters is entering the top 10, and ideally the top 3, for terms buyers use.
- **Whether the report shows the same keywords every month.** If the keyword list quietly changes each month, the agency may be dropping terms that fell and adding terms that rose. Ask for the original list, tracked consistently.

## Check 4: Conversions, not just traffic

Traffic is a means, not an end. If organic visits doubled and enquiries stayed the same, the SEO brought the wrong visitors, or the website failed to convert them. Either way, the renewal decision should reflect that.

What to check:

- **Leads, calls, bookings, or sales attributed to organic search.** In Google Analytics, this is conversions with source "google / organic." The report should show this number for the contract period, not just sessions.
- **The conversion rate of organic traffic.** If traffic grew but the conversion rate collapsed, the new visitors are poorly targeted — often the result of chasing high-volume, low-intent keywords.
- **Phone calls and form fills, not only online purchases.** Most Nepali businesses convert offline: a call, a WhatsApp message, a walk-in. If the agency never set up call tracking or form-goal tracking, they have no idea whether SEO produced customers, and neither do you. That missing setup is a finding in itself.

If the report contains no business metric at all — no leads, no calls, no sales — treat that as the biggest red flag in the document. An agency that cannot connect its work to your revenue is asking you to renew on faith.

## Check 5: What work was actually done

A report should list deliverables, not just results. Results without deliverables usually mean the results came from something else — seasonality, your own marketing, or Google's own changes.

What to check:

- **Content published.** A list of pages and posts, with URLs and publish dates. Open a few. Are they genuinely useful, specific to your business, and written for your customers? Or generic filler that could belong to any business in any country?
- **Technical fixes shipped.** The first audit should have listed problems: slow pages, broken links, missing titles, crawl errors. The report should show which were fixed, when, and what changed afterward. "Technical SEO ongoing" without a fix list is not a deliverable.
- **Links earned.** A list of websites that linked to you, with the actual URLs. Open them. Are they real websites in your industry or region? A link from a real Nepali news site, tourism portal, or industry directory is worth more than fifty links from directories nobody has heard of.
- **Google Business Profile work.** For local businesses, this is often where the customers actually come from: posts, photos, review responses, correct hours and services. If the agency claims local SEO, the report should show what changed on the profile.

Vague phrases to watch for: "on-page optimization done," "off-page work completed," "content marketing in progress." These describe nothing verifiable. Ask for the list behind each phrase.

## Check 6: The comparison game

Numbers can be made to say almost anything by choosing the right comparison period. Agencies know this. You should too.

What to check:

- **Year-over-year beats month-over-month.** Comparing this month to last month is noisy: festivals, weather, school holidays, and tourist seasons all move Nepali search demand. Dashain and Tihar reshape shopping and travel searches every year. Comparing this October to last October removes most of that noise.
- **The starting point.** A report that starts its chart at the lowest month of the year will show dramatic growth. Ask to see the chart from before the contract began.
- **Averages that hide decline.** "Average position improved" can hide the fact that your best pages fell while obscure pages rose. Ask for position trends on your top 10 pages specifically.
- **Screenshots instead of live data.** A screenshot of a dashboard can be cropped, dated, or taken from a different property. Ask for access to the live Search Console and Analytics properties, or for exported data you can verify. If the agency set these up under their own account and you have no access, that is a problem to fix before renewing — those accounts should be yours.

## Good report vs. bad report at a glance

| What the report shows | Good report | Bad report |
|---|---|---|
| Clicks | Non-branded clicks rising over the full contract | Only total impressions or total traffic |
| Rankings | Positions for your agreed money terms | Hundreds of keywords nobody searches |
| Business results | Leads, calls, or sales from organic search | No conversion number anywhere |
| Work done | Pages, fixes, and links listed with URLs | "Optimization done" with no list |
| Data access | Live Search Console and Analytics access | Screenshots only, accounts owned by the agency |
| Next period | Specific work planned and priced | Vague promises and a bigger retainer |

## Red flags that mean do not renew

Any one of these is worth a hard conversation. Two or more, and the renewal is very likely a waste of money.

1. **The report has no conversions.** No leads, calls, or sales from organic search anywhere in the document.
2. **All growth is branded.** Non-branded clicks are flat or the agency will not show the split.
3. **Rankings are reported without search demand.** Hundreds of page-one keywords, none of which your customers type.
4. **No deliverables list.** No pages, no fixes, no links — only charts.
5. **The data is unverifiable.** Screenshots only, no access to Search Console or Analytics, or accounts owned by the agency.
6. **Guarantees were made.** Nobody can guarantee a number-one ranking. An agency that promised one either does not understand Google or does not respect you.
7. **You have never spoken to the person doing the work.** Monthly reports with no calls, no explanations, and no strategy discussion usually mean the account is on autopilot.
8. **Traffic grew but revenue did not, and nobody investigated why.** That is the agency telling you, through inaction, that your business results are not their metric.

## Questions to ask before you sign again

Take these into the renewal meeting. The quality of the answers tells you as much as the report does.

- Which specific search terms that our customers use moved into the top 10 during this contract?
- How many leads, calls, or sales came from organic search, and how is that tracked?
- Show me the non-branded click trend for the full contract period.
- What exactly will you do differently in the next 6 months, and what will it cost?
- Which pages did you publish, which technical issues did you fix, and which links did you earn — with URLs?
- If we stop, what happens? Do we keep the content, the links, and the accounts? Is anything rented, such as links that disappear when payment stops?
- Who owns the Search Console and Analytics properties, and will you transfer them to us?

An agency doing good work welcomes these questions — the answers are its best sales pitch. An agency doing weak work deflects them. Notice which one you are dealing with.

## What good looks like

A renewal-worthy SEO engagement has a shape you can recognize:

- Non-branded clicks rising steadily over the contract, with normal ups and downs.
- Rankings improving for terms your customers use, including competitive ones.
- A clear list of published content, shipped fixes, and earned links, all verifiable.
- Conversions tracked and reported, with honest discussion when a channel underperforms.
- Full access to your own data, and a plan for the next period that names specific work, not vague promises.

Renewals should be earned with evidence, not with a nice PDF. The agencies worth keeping know this, and their reports read like proof, not like marketing.

## Honest tradeoffs

This way of judging a report has limits. It needs a contract of at least six months to be fair — three months of data can look flat even when the work is sound. It also needs the agency's cooperation: if they refuse to share Search Console access or the branded/non-branded split, you cannot complete the checks, and that refusal is itself the answer. And a report can pass every check here while still being the wrong fit — if the agency's strategy targets the wrong audience, good numbers on the wrong terms will not grow your business. The checks prove the work is real; they cannot prove the strategy is right. That is a separate conversation, and it is one worth having before you renew.

## Final recommendation

Do not renew on the strength of the report's design or its biggest number. Run the five checks — non-branded clicks, rankings on your money terms, conversions, the deliverables list, and account ownership — and renew only if the evidence holds up on at least four of them. If it holds up on fewer, renegotiate with specific demands (the split, the list, the access) and a shorter term. If the agency will not provide them, walk away: the money is better spent with someone whose report reads like proof.

## FAQs

**How long should I give SEO before judging it?**
For most Nepali businesses, six months is a fair window to see a clear trend in non-branded clicks and rankings for target terms. Three months is usually too early to judge fairly — but it is not too early to expect deliverables: published pages, fixed issues, and a visible work plan. If month three has no work to show, month six rarely surprises.

**My agency says SEO takes time and I should be patient. Are they right?**
Sometimes. SEO genuinely compounds slowly, and competitive terms can take many months. But "be patient" is also the standard defence of doing nothing. The difference is evidence: a patient-worthy agency shows rising non-branded clicks, shipped work, and improving positions on real terms, even if the business results are still building. Patience without evidence is just paying longer.

**Should I compare my agency's report with a second opinion?**
Yes, and it is cheap insurance before a renewal. A one-off audit from an independent SEO professional — someone with no interest in winning your retainer — will usually confirm or contradict the report within a few days. Bring the report and grant read-only access to Search Console and Analytics.

**What if my traffic dropped? Is that always the agency's fault?**
Not always. Google updates, new competitors, seasonal demand, and website changes outside the agency's control can all cause drops. What matters is whether the report explains the drop honestly, identifies the cause, and names the recovery plan. A drop with a credible explanation and a plan is manageable. A drop buried in averages and jargon is not.

**Can I do SEO myself instead of renewing?**
For a small business with one location and a simple website, the basics — a complete Google Business Profile, service pages for every offering, fast loading pages, and honest content answering customer questions — are genuinely doable in-house. What an agency should add is capacity, technical depth, and consistency. If your agency is not adding those three things, the renewal money may work harder elsewhere.

## Bottom line

Before you renew, check five things: non-branded clicks over the full contract, rankings for terms your customers actually search, conversions from organic traffic, a verifiable list of work done, and who owns the accounts and content. If the report proves progress on those five, renewing is an easy decision. If it leans on impressions, branded traffic, and vague claims, you are being sold a story, not a result — and the money is better spent with someone whose report reads like proof.
