Most Nepali business websites fail at SEO before they ever publish a second page. Not because of algorithms, backlinks, or competition — but because the basics on the page itself are wrong. Missing title tags, one giant paragraph of text, stock-photo headings that say nothing, and zero internal links. These are free to fix, take hours rather than months, and they are the foundation every higher-level SEO tactic builds on.

This guide covers the on-page SEO basics every Nepali business — restaurant, hotel, clinic, shop, or service provider in Kathmandu Valley and beyond — should get right on every page. It is for owners and marketers who want the exact checklist, in plain language. It is **not** for developers who want code-level implementation details, and it is not a substitute for a full [technical SEO audit](/blog/technical-seo-audit-checklist-nepal) — but it is the place most Nepali sites should start.

## TL;DR

- **On-page SEO** means optimizing what is on the page itself: titles, headings, content, images, and links. You control all of it, and it costs nothing.
- Every page needs **one clear topic**, a title tag that names it, and headings that match what customers actually search.
- Write content that **answers the question behind the search** — not filler, not keyword stuffing. Short paragraphs, real details, real prices and locations where possible.
- **Internal links** between your pages are the most ignored basic on Nepali sites. Link your service pages to each other with descriptive text.
- Fix the basics on your most important pages first: homepage, service pages, and your top traffic pages. One afternoon of work beats a month of waiting for backlinks.

## Why On-Page Basics Matter More for Nepali Businesses

In mature markets, on-page basics are table stakes — everyone has them, so they rarely decide rankings. In Nepal, the field is thinner. Many local business websites were built once, years ago, by a developer who moved on, and have barely changed since. A large share of them share the same problems: no title tags, copied-and-pasted content, English headings on pages Nepali customers search in different words, and no links between pages.

That weakness is your opportunity. When your competitors' pages are poorly optimized, getting the basics right is often enough to outrank them — no expensive link-building campaigns required. And unlike backlinks, on-page SEO is entirely in your hands. You do not need permission, a budget, or an agency. You need an afternoon, this checklist, and honest content about your business.

There is a second reason on-page matters especially here: **language**. Many Nepali customers search in a mix of English and Nepali — "dentist in Kathmandu," "सस्तो होटल पोखरा," or Romanized Nepali like "sasto hotel pokhara." Your on-page basics decide whether your pages match any of those searches. That is a content decision you make on the page, not something backlinks fix.

## The On-Page Checklist: What to Get Right on Every Page

### 1. One page, one topic, one search intent

Before anything else, each page on your site should answer **one question from one type of searcher**. A page titled "Services" that lists dentistry, braces, implants, and teeth whitening in four bullet points ranks for none of them. A page titled "Dental Implants in Kathmandu — Cost, Process, and Recovery" can rank for dental-implant searches.

This is the single most common on-page mistake I see on Nepali business sites: one thin "Services" page trying to rank for ten different things, while competitors with dedicated pages for each service take every search.

**What to do:** List your main services or topics. Give each one its own page with its own title and content. If a page cannot be summarized in one sentence that a customer would search for, split it.

### 2. Title tags that earn the click

The title tag is the blue headline people see in Google's results. It is the single most important on-page element, and on many Nepali sites it says something useless like "Home," "Welcome," or just the business name.

A good title tag names the **topic + location + business**, and reads like something a human would click:

- Bad: "Home"
- Bad: "ABC Dental Care"
- Good: "Dentist in Kathmandu | ABC Dental Care"
- Good: "Dental Implants in Kathmandu — Cost & Process | ABC Dental"

Keep it under 60 characters so Google does not cut it off. Put the most important words first — "Dentist in Kathmandu" before the brand name — because that is what the searcher is looking for.

**What to do:** Open your homepage and your top 5 pages. If any title is "Home," "Welcome," or just your brand name, rewrite it today using the formula: *what the page is about + where + brand*.

### 3. Headings that structure the page (H1, H2, H3)

Headings do two jobs: they tell Google what each section is about, and they let visitors scan the page instead of reading every word. Most Nepali sites fail at one or both — either a page has no headings at all (one long wall of text), or every heading is a design flourish like "We Care About Excellence" that tells Google nothing.

The rules are simple:

- **One H1 per page.** It should match the page's topic closely — similar to the title tag, but written as a page headline, not a search result. For a dentist's implant page: "Dental Implants in Kathmandu."
- **H2s for the main sections.** These are your sub-topics: cost, the procedure, recovery time, FAQs. Each H2 should contain real keywords a customer would use — "Dental Implant Cost in Nepal," not "Investment Details."
- **H3s for subsections** within an H2, used sparingly.
- Never skip levels (no H1 straight to H3), and never use headings just to make text bigger. If it is not a section title, it is not a heading.

**What to do:** On your key pages, check: one H1 that names the topic, H2s that answer the questions customers ask. Rewrite any heading that could describe literally any business ("Welcome," "Our Services," "Why Choose Us") into something specific ("Teeth Cleaning & Polishing in Kathmandu").

### 4. Content that answers, not content that fills

Google ranks pages that **satisfy the search**. That sounds obvious, but most Nepali business pages were written to fill space: three generic sentences about "quality service" and a contact button. That content cannot rank, because it answers nothing a searcher asked.

Good on-page content has a few traits:

- **It answers the question behind the search.** Someone searching "trekking gear shop in Thamel" wants to know what you sell, where you are, your hours, and your prices. Say all of that, on the page, in the first screen.
- **It uses the customer's words.** A clinic should write "root canal treatment cost in Kathmandu," not "endodontic therapy pricing." Write how customers talk, not how professionals talk.
- **It has real detail.** Prices or price ranges, locations, hours, process steps, timelines. Generic claims ("best quality," "affordable prices") are invisible to Google and unconvincing to humans. Specifics rank.
- **It is long enough to cover the topic.** For a service page, that usually means 600–1,200 words of genuinely useful content — not padding. A 150-word page cannot answer a customer's questions.
- **Short paragraphs, clear language.** Two to four sentences per paragraph. If a sentence needs re-reading, shorten it.

One thing to avoid: **keyword stuffing.** Repeating "dentist in Kathmandu" fifteen times does not help; it reads as spam to Google and to visitors. Use the phrase naturally — in the title, the H1, once or twice in the body, and in a couple of H2s — and then write for humans.

### 5. Images with purpose (and alt text)

Images matter for on-page SEO in two ways: they keep visitors on the page (a page of pure text loses people), and their alt text gives Google another signal about the page's topic. Common mistakes: stock photos that could be any business anywhere, and images uploaded as "IMG_2041.jpg" with no alt text.

- **Use real photos** of your business, your work, your team. Real photos convert better and differentiate you from every competitor using the same stock images.
- **Name files descriptively:** dental-clinic-thamel-reception.jpg, not IMG_2041.jpg.
- **Write alt text** that describes the image in the page's context: "Dental clinic reception in Thamel, Kathmandu." Keep it under 125 characters.
- **Compress images** before uploading. Heavy images slow the page, and slow pages lose rankings — especially on Nepal's mobile connections, where most of your customers browse.

### 6. Internal links: the most ignored basic

Internal links — links from one page of your site to another — tell Google which pages matter and how they relate. On most Nepali business sites, the only internal links are the main navigation menu. The service pages do not link to each other, blog posts link nowhere, and the homepage links only to the top-level pages.

This is free authority you are leaving on the table. Fix it like this:

- **Link related pages to each other.** Your dental implants page should link to your teeth cleaning page and your pricing page, using descriptive anchor text like "see our teeth cleaning services in Kathmandu." Your restaurant's menu page should link to your reservation and location pages.
- **Use descriptive anchor text.** "Click here" tells Google nothing. "Dental implant cost in Nepal" tells Google exactly what the linked page is about.
- **Link from high-traffic pages to important pages.** Your homepage and your most-visited blog posts should link to the pages that bring you customers — your service and contact pages.
- **Link new content to old content.** Every time you publish a blog post, link it to two or three related existing pages, and add a link from the most relevant existing page back to the new post.

A site with 20 pages and no internal links is 20 isolated islands. A site with 20 pages linked thoughtfully is a network — and Google rewards networks.

### 7. URLs that say what the page is

Keep URLs short, readable, and keyword-relevant: `/dental-implants-kathmandu/`, not `/services.php?id=7`. If your site already has ugly URLs that rank or have links pointing to them, do not change them without setting up redirects — but use clean URLs for every new page.

### 8. Meta descriptions: your free ad copy

The meta description is the short text under your title in Google's results. It does not directly affect rankings, but it affects whether people **click** — and more clicks at the same ranking position means more customers without more SEO work.

Write one for every important page: 140–160 characters, one sentence describing the page plus one reason to click. Example: "Dental implants in Kathmandu from NPR X. Painless procedure, lifetime warranty, free consultation. Book your appointment today."

## The Priority Order: What to Fix First

Do not try to fix every page at once. Work in this order:

1. **Homepage.** It is usually your most-visited and most-linked page. One H1, a clear title tag, content that says what you do and where, links to your main service pages.
2. **Service/product pages.** One page per service, each with its own title, headings, and real content. This is where most Nepali sites have the biggest gap.
3. **Contact/location page.** Name, address, phone, hours, a map, and the areas you serve — written out in text, not just embedded in an image. This feeds your local rankings directly.
4. **Top blog posts or content pages.** Fix titles, headings, and internal links on the pages already getting traffic.
5. **Everything else**, page by page, over the following weeks.

One focused afternoon on your homepage and top service pages will do more for your rankings than most of the "SEO packages" being sold in Kathmandu. I say that as someone who does this for a living: the basics are unglamorous, but they are the highest-return work in SEO, because so few competitors have done them.

## My Approach: Measurement Before Guesswork

When I take on on-page work for a client, I do not start by rewriting pages. I start by measuring: which pages get impressions but no clicks (usually a title/meta description problem), which pages rank on page two (usually a content-depth and internal-linking problem), and which pages Google is not indexing at all (usually a technical problem, not on-page).

That measurement-first habit is part of why a client ecommerce site I worked on grew from near-zero visibility to **265K Google Search impressions and 6.76K clicks in 8 months** — the fixes went to the pages where the data said they would matter, instead of being spread evenly across the site. You can apply the same discipline yourself: open Google Search Console, sort pages by impressions, and start your on-page fixes at the top of that list.

## Honest Tradeoffs

- **On-page SEO is necessary but not sufficient.** Perfect on-page work on a brand-new site with zero authority will still lose to a mediocre page on a trusted, well-linked site. On-page gets you into the fight; authority and content depth win it.
- **It takes time to show.** On-page changes typically need 2–8 weeks to reflect in rankings, because Google must re-crawl and re-evaluate each page. Do not rewrite a page, check rankings the next day, and rewrite it again. For realistic expectations, read [how long SEO takes for Nepali businesses](/blog/how-long-does-seo-take-nepal).
- **Thin content cannot be optimized.** If a page has 100 words of generic text, no title tag rewrite will save it. Sometimes the honest fix is to write the page properly, not to tweak it.
- **DIY has limits.** The checklist above covers what a business owner can do. Site architecture, redirect strategy, and large-scale internal linking on big sites are where working with an [SEO expert in Nepal](/seo-expert-in-nepal) starts to pay for itself.

## FAQs

### What is on-page SEO, in simple terms?

On-page SEO is everything you optimize on the page itself to help it rank: the title tag, headings, content, images, internal links, URLs, and meta descriptions. It is called "on-page" because it happens on your pages, as opposed to off-page SEO (like backlinks) which happens elsewhere. It is the part of SEO you fully control, and it costs nothing to do.

### How many keywords should I target per page?

One primary keyword (the main topic) plus a handful of closely related phrases. A page about "dental implants in Kathmandu" can naturally include "dental implant cost in Nepal," "implant dentist Kathmandu," and "tooth replacement options." If you find yourself targeting ten unrelated keywords on one page, you need ten pages — or at least several.

### Do I need to write in Nepali, English, or both?

Write in the language your customers search in. For most professional services in Kathmandu, that is English — "dentist in Kathmandu" gets far more searches than its Nepali equivalent. For mass-market products and local services, test Nepali and Romanized Nepali phrases too. Check Google's autocomplete and "People also ask" for your topic in both languages; the suggestions show you what people actually type.

### How long should my service pages be?

Long enough to genuinely answer a customer's questions — usually 600 to 1,200 words for a service page. Length is not the goal; completeness is. A 600-word page that covers cost, process, timeline, and FAQs outranks a 2,000-word page of padded generic text. If you are unsure, look at the pages currently ranking for your keyword and match their depth.

### Can on-page SEO alone get me to page one?

Sometimes, yes — in low-competition niches where competitors have weak pages, strong on-page work alone can win. In competitive niches (hotels in Pokhara, restaurants in Thamel), on-page SEO is the entry ticket: you need it, but you also need content depth, reviews, local signals, and links. Think of it as the foundation, not the whole house.

### How often should I update my pages?

Review your important pages every 6–12 months. Update prices, add new FAQs based on questions customers actually ask, refresh examples, and improve anything competitors now do better. Google favors pages that stay current, and customers trust pages that look maintained. A page untouched since 2021 quietly loses to one updated last month.

### Should I hire someone for on-page SEO or do it myself?

Do the basics yourself using this checklist — title tags, headings, content improvements, and internal links are all learnable in a weekend, and nobody knows your business better than you. Hire help when the site is large (dozens of pages), when technical issues are mixed in with content issues, or when you have done the basics and rankings have stalled.
