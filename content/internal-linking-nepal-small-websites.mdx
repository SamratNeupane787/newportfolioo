Most small Nepali websites have a hidden problem: pages Google barely finds, pages that get no traffic even though they answer real questions, and a homepage that holds all the authority while the rest of the site starves. Internal linking fixes all three — and it costs nothing. If your site has more than five pages, this is the highest-return SEO work you can do this week.

**Who this is for:** owners of small Nepali business websites — hotels, restaurants, clinics, shops, service businesses — with at least a handful of pages that should be bringing in customers but aren't. **Who it is NOT for:** brand-new one-page websites with nothing to link between, or sites already ranking well across their key pages.

## TL;DR

- **Internal links are links from one page on your site to another page on your site.** They tell Google which pages exist, which ones matter most, and how pages relate to each other.
- **Most small sites underuse them badly.** Service pages sit orphaned with zero links pointing to them, blog posts never link back to the pages that make money, and the homepage hoards all the authority.
- **The fix is a simple system, not a tool:** map your important pages, link to them from your strongest pages with descriptive anchor text, and repeat every time you publish.
- **Expect movement in weeks, not months** — especially on pages that already rank on page two or three. Internal linking rarely creates rankings from nothing, but it regularly lifts pages that are close.
- **Don't overdo it.** A page stuffed with fifty links helps nobody. Ten to twenty useful, relevant links per page is plenty for most small sites.

## What Internal Linking Actually Is

An internal link is any link that takes a visitor from one page of your website to another page of the same website. The "Book a Room" button on your homepage that goes to your rooms page is an internal link. The line in a blog post that says "see our [trekking packages](/blog/seo-for-hotels-tourism-nepal)" is an internal link. Your main menu is a set of internal links.

That sounds too simple to matter. It matters because of how Google works. Google discovers pages by following links, and it judges how important a page is partly by how many links point to it — including links from your own site. A page with no internal links pointing to it is called an **orphan page**, and Google may not find it at all, or may find it and assume it is unimportant because even your own website doesn't link to it.

Think of your website as a small town. Your homepage is the main square — everyone arrives there. Internal links are the roads. If a page has no roads leading to it, nobody visits, and Google's crawler — which travels by following roads — may never arrive either.

## Why It Matters More Than Most Nepali Businesses Realise

**1. It helps Google find and index your pages.** New pages on small sites often sit undiscovered for weeks. A link from a page Google already crawls regularly — usually your homepage or a popular blog post — gets the new page found fast. When I publish a new post on this site, I link to it from related posts, and it shows up in search far sooner than it would on its own.

**2. It passes authority where it is needed.** Your homepage usually has the most authority on your site, because that is where most other websites link to. Internal links let you share that authority with the pages that actually earn you money — your services, your booking page, your product categories. A trekking company's homepage might attract links naturally, but the page for the Annapurna Base Camp trek is the one that needs to rank. Internal links move the value from one to the other.

**3. It tells Google what each page is about.** The clickable words in a link — the anchor text — are a strong signal. A link that says "click here" tells Google nothing. A link that says "SEO services in Nepal" tells Google exactly what the target page covers. This is one of the few ranking signals you control completely.

**4. It keeps visitors on your site longer.** A visitor reading your blog post about [choosing an SEO expert](/blog/how-to-choose-seo-expert-in-nepal) who sees a natural link to your services page is one click from becoming an enquiry. Without that link, they finish the article and leave. Internal links turn readers into customers by putting the next step in front of them at the right moment.

## Internal Links vs Backlinks: What's the Difference?

| | Internal links | Backlinks |
|---|---|---|
| **What they are** | Links between pages on your own site | Links from other websites to yours |
| **Who controls them** | You, completely | Other site owners — you can only earn or request them |
| **Cost** | Free | Time, relationships, or content investment |
| **Main job** | Help Google find and value your pages; guide visitors | Build your site's overall authority and trust |
| **Risk** | Almost none, if relevant and natural | High if bought or spammy — Google penalises link schemes |

You need both. But backlinks are slow and partly out of your control, while internal links are entirely in your hands and free. That is why internal linking is the most underused lever: businesses chase the hard thing and ignore the easy one sitting in front of them.

## The 5-Step Internal Linking System for a Small Site

You don't need an expensive tool. A spreadsheet and an hour will do.

### Step 1: List your money pages

Write down the 5–10 pages that actually earn you business: your main services, your booking or contact page, your best product categories, your location pages. These are the pages your internal links should support first. Everything else on the site exists, in part, to point at them.

### Step 2: Find your strongest pages

These are the pages Google already likes: your homepage, your most-visited blog posts, any page that already ranks on page one or two. You can find them in Google Search Console — look at the pages with the most impressions and clicks. These pages have authority to share, so they are where your most valuable links should come from.

### Step 3: Connect them with descriptive anchor text

From each strong page, add natural links to your money pages where a reader would genuinely find them useful. Use anchor text that describes the destination: "our [hotel SEO guide](/blog/seo-for-hotels-tourism-nepal)" beats "click here" every time. The link should make sense in the sentence — if it feels forced, it will feel forced to readers and to Google too.

### Step 4: Link new content the day you publish it

Every new page or post should launch with internal links in both directions: the new page should link out to two or three relevant existing pages, and at least one or two existing pages should link to the new one. A page published with no links pointing to it starts life as an orphan. Make this a habit and your site's structure stays healthy by itself. This is exactly the routine I follow on this blog — each new post links to related older posts, and older posts get updated to link forward when a new one is relevant.

### Step 5: Check for orphans every few months

Open Google Search Console and compare the pages it has indexed with the pages on your site. Any page that exists but never appears in search results may be orphaned or buried too deep — more than three clicks from your homepage. Add links to it from a stronger page and watch what happens over the next few weeks.

## Anchor Text: The Rules That Actually Matter

- **Be descriptive.** "SEO reporting for Nepali businesses" tells Google and readers what to expect. "This article" tells them nothing.
- **Vary it.** Don't use the exact same anchor text for the same page fifty times. Natural variation — "SEO services", "our SEO packages", "how our SEO service works" — looks human because it is human.
- **Don't stuff keywords.** Repeating your exact target keyword in every link to a page looks manipulative. Google has penalised that behaviour for years. If the anchor reads naturally in the sentence, it is fine.
- **Match the link to the reader's intent.** Someone reading about [how long SEO takes](/blog/how-long-does-seo-take-nepal) is ready to hear about services. Someone reading a recipe on a restaurant blog is not ready for a sales page — link them to another recipe instead.

## Common Internal Linking Mistakes

| Mistake | Why it hurts | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Orphan pages with no links in | Google may never find or value them | Link to every page from at least one indexed page |
| Every link says "click here" | Wastes the anchor-text signal | Describe the destination in the link text |
| Linking only to the homepage | Authority pools at the top and never reaches money pages | Link deep — to services, products, and key posts |
| Footer stuffed with keyword links | Looks spammy; Google largely ignores footer links | Keep the footer for navigation; put real links in the body |
| Pages buried 4+ clicks deep | Google crawls deep pages rarely and values them less | Flatten the structure — important pages within 2–3 clicks of home |
| Broken internal links | Dead ends frustrate visitors and waste crawl budget | Check for 404s in Search Console and fix them |
| No links from blog posts to services | Your best content never converts readers | Add one natural, relevant service link per post |

## How Many Internal Links Should a Page Have?

There is no magic number, and anyone selling you one is guessing. A useful guide for a small site: **a typical content page should link out to 5–15 other pages** on your site, all of them relevant to what the reader is reading. Your homepage will naturally have more, because it links to everything through navigation. A short service page might have three or four, and that is fine.

The test is simple: would a real visitor find this link helpful? If yes, keep it. If you added it only for Google, remove it. Google's guidance is consistent on this point — links exist for users first, and the ranking benefit follows from that.

## Honest Tradeoffs

Internal linking is powerful, but it has limits, and you should know them before you start:

- **It won't rescue bad content.** Links can lift a decent page that Google undervalues. They cannot make a thin, unhelpful page rank for a competitive keyword. Fix the page first.
- **It won't replace backlinks.** Internal links distribute the authority your site already has. They don't create new authority. A site with zero backlinks still needs to earn some from the outside world.
- **It takes maintenance.** Sites grow messy. Pages get deleted and links break. A quarterly check — an hour with Search Console — keeps the structure clean. Skip it for a year and the orphans pile up again.
- **Results are gradual.** Expect movement in two to six weeks for pages that are already close to ranking, and slower for everything else. Internal linking is a compounding habit, not a one-time trick.

## FAQs

### How do I find orphan pages on my website?

Start in Google Search Console: open the Pages report and compare the list of indexed pages with the pages you know exist on your site. Any live page that never appears is a candidate orphan. Then check your own navigation and content — if you can't reach a page by clicking links from your homepage within three clicks, and no blog post links to it, it is effectively orphaned. Add a link from a relevant, well-visited page and the page should appear in search within a few weeks.

### Should I link to a page from every other page on my site?

No. Sitewide links — the same link repeated in a sidebar or footer on every page — carry very little value after the first one, and a footer stuffed with keyword links looks spammy. What matters is links inside the main content of a page, placed where a reader would naturally want them. One thoughtful in-content link beats twenty footer links.

### Is it bad to have too many internal links on one page?

Only if they stop being useful. A page with fifty links to loosely related pages dilutes the value each link passes and overwhelms the reader. A page with ten links that all genuinely help the reader is fine. The practical rule is to link where it helps and stop when it doesn't. Most small-site pages never come close to "too many" — the usual problem is far too few.

### Do internal links help a new website rank faster?

Yes, more than almost anything else a new site can do for free. A new site's pages get discovered through links, and its homepage is usually the only page with any authority. Linking new pages from the homepage and from your best existing content gets them crawled and indexed sooner, and gives them a share of whatever authority the site has. It won't replace the need for good content and real backlinks over time, but it makes everything else work faster.

### Should anchor text always include my target keyword?

No — and forcing it is one of the most common mistakes. Use the keyword when it fits naturally in the sentence, and use variations the rest of the time: partial phrases, brand terms, or plain descriptions like "our pricing page" or "the full checklist". Google reads the words around the link as well as the link itself, so natural writing gives it plenty of signal. A page with every inbound link using identical keyword anchor text looks manipulated, not optimised.

### How often should I review my internal links?

For a small site, once a quarter is enough — an hour with Google Search Console and a look at your newest pages. Do a quick check every time you publish something new (link it in both directions, as in step 4 above), and a deeper review when you redesign, delete pages, or change your services. If rankings for an important page slip, broken or lost internal links are one of the first things worth checking.
