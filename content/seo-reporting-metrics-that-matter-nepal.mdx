If you pay for SEO in Nepal, you probably get a monthly report. And if you are like most business owners I talk to, you open it, glance at a few big numbers, and close it — because nothing in it tells you whether the money is actually working.

That is not your fault. Most SEO reports are built to impress, not to inform. A report full of rising graphs can hide a simple truth: your phone is not ringing more, your bookings are not up, and your sales have not moved.

This guide is for any Nepali business owner who pays for SEO — or is thinking about it — and wants to read a report like someone who knows what to look for. It is **not** for SEO professionals looking for advanced analytics setups.

## TL;DR

- **Vanity metrics** look good in a report but do not connect to money: total traffic, raw impressions, keyword counts, and domain authority scores.
- **Metrics that matter** connect search to business: organic clicks, conversions (calls, form fills, bookings, sales), revenue from organic search, and rankings for keywords your customers actually use.
- A good report shows **what changed, why it changed, and what happens next** — not just a pile of charts.
- Ask your SEO provider one question every month: **"What did this month's work earn us?"** If they cannot answer it, the report is decoration.

## Why most SEO reports in Nepal mislead

SEO reporting in Nepal has a trust problem, and it starts with incentives. Many providers are paid a flat monthly retainer, and the easiest way to keep a client happy is a report full of green arrows. Traffic is up. Impressions are up. Keywords are up. Everything is up — except, somehow, your business.

The numbers are usually real. The problem is what they leave out. A report can show traffic growing 40% while your enquiries stay flat, because the new traffic comes from keywords nobody buys from — informational searches, people in other countries, or terms so broad they will never convert. In Nepal, where many businesses serve a local market in Kathmandu, Pokhara, or Chitwan, a thousand extra visitors from the wrong place are worth less than ten visitors who were ready to call.

## Vanity metrics vs metrics that matter

Every metric below is a real number. The difference is whether it helps you decide anything.

| Metric | Vanity or value? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Total website traffic | Mostly vanity | Counts every visitor, including bots, irrelevant countries, and people who will never buy. A rise here can mean nothing. |
| Total impressions | Vanity on its own | Impressions mean your pages appeared in search results — not that anyone clicked, called, or bought. |
| Number of keywords ranking | Vanity on its own | Ranking for 500 keywords sounds great until you learn 480 of them bring zero clicks. |
| Domain authority / domain rating | Vanity | Third-party scores, not Google's. They can be gamed, and they do not pay your bills. |
| Bounce rate (alone) | Vanity | A visitor can read your phone number, call you, and "bounce." The metric cannot see the phone call. |
| Organic clicks | **Value** | Real people choosing your page in real search results. The closest thing to foot traffic. |
| Click-through rate (CTR) | **Value** | Shows whether your titles and descriptions actually win the click — and whether you rank for searches that matter. |
| Average position for money keywords | **Value** | Your ranking for the specific searches your customers use when they are ready to buy, not for every keyword you rank for. |
| Conversions from organic search | **Value** | Calls, form submissions, bookings, WhatsApp messages, and sales that came from search. This is the metric that justifies the retainer. |
| Revenue or leads from organic search | **Value** | The only number that answers "was this worth it?" If your provider cannot connect SEO to revenue or leads, you are buying hope. |

The rule is simple: **if a metric cannot be traced to a customer action, treat it as decoration until proven otherwise.**

## The metrics that matter, explained

### 1. Organic clicks — your real foot traffic

In Google Search Console, clicks count the times someone actually visited your site from a Google search. Impressions are how often you appeared; clicks are how often someone chose you.

When I grew a client ecommerce site from near-zero visibility to **265K Google Search impressions and 6.76K clicks in 8 months**, the impressions were the headline — but the clicks were the proof. Impressions without clicks mean you are visible and ignored.

### 2. Average position — for the right keywords only

Average position tells you where your pages typically appear in search results. A move from position 18 to position 7 for "best hotel in Pokhara" is worth far more than a move from position 40 to position 9 for a keyword nobody searches.

Ask your provider to report positions for a short, agreed list of **money keywords** — the searches your customers type when they are ready to buy, book, or call. Ten keywords that matter beat five hundred that do not. If you are not sure which keywords those are, start with my guide to [keyword research for the Nepal market](/blog/keyword-research-nepal-english-vs-nepali-search).

### 3. Click-through rate — are you winning the click?

CTR is clicks divided by impressions. If your page appears 1,000 times and gets 10 clicks, your CTR is 1% — and something is wrong with your title, your description, or your relevance to that search. A low CTR on a page that ranks well is one of the cheapest wins in SEO: better titles and descriptions can lift clicks without any new rankings at all.

### 4. Conversions — the metric that justifies the retainer

A conversion is a visitor doing the thing your business needs: calling, filling a form, booking a table, sending a WhatsApp message, or buying. This is where most Nepali SEO reports go quiet, because conversions require setup — call tracking, form tracking, or at minimum a thank-you page that analytics can count.

If your report has no conversion data, ask for it before renewing. Without it, neither you nor your provider can prove the work is paying off. For a restaurant, a conversion might be a table booking or a call; for a hotel, a booking enquiry; for a clinic, an appointment request. My guide to [SEO for restaurants in Kathmandu](/blog/seo-for-restaurants-in-kathmandu) shows what local conversions look like in practice.

### 5. Revenue and leads from organic search

This is the final step: connecting conversions to money. Not every business can track this perfectly — a customer may find you on Google and call instead of clicking — but you can get close. Ask new customers how they found you and track it in a simple sheet. Over a few months, a pattern appears, and it is usually clearer than any analytics dashboard.

If your SEO costs NPR 15,000 a month and organic search brings in even two or three extra customers a month, the math usually works. If it brings in none, no amount of green arrows changes that.

## How to read your monthly SEO report in 10 minutes

You do not need to become an SEO expert to judge a report. Work through this checklist every month:

1. **Clicks vs last month and vs three months ago.** Is the trend up, flat, or down? One good month means little; three months show a direction.
2. **Which pages earned the clicks?** Your homepage and your money pages (services, products, booking) should be near the top. If all the growth is on one old blog post, the business pages are not pulling their weight.
3. **Which keywords moved?** Look for movement on your money keywords, not the total keyword count.
4. **How many conversions came from organic search?** Calls, forms, bookings, sales — the number and the trend.
5. **What work was done this month?** The report should name specific pages fixed, content published, or technical issues resolved. Vague lines like "ongoing optimization" are not work you can verify.
6. **What happens next month?** A real plan names the next pages, keywords, or fixes — not "continue SEO efforts."

## Red flags in an SEO report

One red flag on its own is a warning; several together mean you should ask hard questions before renewing:

- **Only traffic and impressions, no conversions.** The provider is reporting what is easy, not what matters.
- **No comparison to previous months.** A single month's numbers, with no trend, hide whether things are improving or drifting.
- **Keyword counts without naming the keywords.** "You now rank for 300 keywords" means nothing if the keywords are irrelevant.
- **Screenshots instead of live data.** A report you cannot verify against Google Search Console or Google Analytics is a claim, not evidence. Ask for access to the real accounts — they are yours.
- **Blaming every drop on Google updates.** Algorithm updates are real, but they are not the explanation for every decline. Ask what specifically changed and what the response is.
- **No mention of your business results.** If the report never mentions calls, bookings, or sales, the provider is not thinking about your business.

## What a good SEO report looks like

A report worth paying for has four parts, and none of them is complicated:

1. **The business summary first.** Leads, calls, bookings, or sales from organic search this month — before any charts.
2. **The trend, not the snapshot.** Clicks, conversions, and positions for money keywords over three to six months, so you can see direction.
3. **The work, named.** Exactly what was done: pages published, technical fixes shipped, pages improved. If you cannot verify the work, you cannot value it.
4. **The next step, specific.** What will be done next month and what result it is expected to produce.

That is the standard I hold my own work to. SEO is a measurement-first discipline: every change should be trackable, and every report should connect the work to the result.

## Honest tradeoffs

Good reporting has limits, and you should know them before you judge any provider by it:

- **SEO results are slow.** A new site or a competitive keyword can take three to six months to show real movement in clicks and conversions. A report that promises fast conversions in month one is promising something SEO cannot deliver. My guide on [how long SEO takes in Nepal](/blog/how-long-does-seo-take-nepal) sets realistic timelines.
- **Attribution is imperfect.** A customer may find you on Google, check your Facebook page, and call a week later. No report captures that journey perfectly. Direction and trend matter more than exact numbers.
- **Some metrics need setup.** Conversion tracking, call tracking, and revenue attribution take time to install and a few weeks of data before they mean anything. If your provider has never set them up, that is a gap to fix — not a reason to keep reporting traffic alone.

## FAQs

### What is the most important SEO metric for a small business in Nepal?

Conversions from organic search — the calls, form fills, bookings, or sales that came from Google. Clicks and rankings tell you whether the work is moving in the right direction; conversions tell you whether it is paying. If you can only watch one number, watch conversions.

### My traffic is up but my sales are flat. What is wrong?

Almost always one of three things. First, the new traffic comes from the wrong keywords — informational searches from people who were never going to buy. Second, the traffic lands on the wrong pages — blog posts instead of your service, booking, or product pages. Third, the pages themselves do not convert — no clear phone number, no booking button, no reason to act now. Check which pages earned the traffic and whether those pages ask the visitor to do anything.

### How often should I get an SEO report?

Monthly, at minimum. Weekly is usually noise — the numbers swing too much to mean anything. Monthly reporting with a three-month trend view is the right rhythm: frequent enough to catch problems early, long enough to see real direction. And you should have live access to Google Search Console and Google Analytics yourself, so you can check the numbers any day without waiting for the report.

### What should I ask my SEO provider before renewing?

Five questions: How many conversions did organic search bring us in the last three months? Which keywords moved, and are they the ones our customers use? What exact work did you do last month? What will you do next month, and what result do you expect? And can I see the live data in Search Console myself? If the answers are vague on two or more of these, think carefully before renewing — and if you are choosing a provider for the first time, start with my guide on [how to choose the right SEO expert in Nepal](/blog/how-to-choose-seo-expert-in-nepal).

### How do I know if my SEO provider is actually doing the work?

Ask for the work, not the summary. Which pages were published or changed? Which technical issues were fixed? You can verify most of it yourself: check whether the new pages exist, whether the site loads faster, whether the reported fixes are visible. A provider doing real work can point to real changes. A provider doing nothing will give you charts instead — which is why my [technical SEO audit checklist](/blog/technical-seo-audit-checklist-nepal) is useful even if you never run an audit yourself: it shows what real SEO work looks like.
