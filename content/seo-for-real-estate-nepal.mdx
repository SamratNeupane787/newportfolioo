## TL;DR

- Property buyers in Nepal search Google before they call anyone — "flats for sale in Kathmandu", "land for sale in Pokhara", "house for rent in Lalitpur" are buying-intent searches, and the agents who rank for them get the enquiry first.
- Real estate SEO is not one page. It is a system: one page per property type and area, fast listing pages that work on mobile, a Google Business Profile that matches your site, and reviews that prove you close deals.
- Portals rent you attention. Your own site builds an asset you own. The smart move is to use both, but never to depend on portals alone.
- Results take months, not days — and the first page that should rank is usually a location page ("flats in Baneshwor"), not your homepage.

## Who this is for

This guide is for real estate agents, brokers, and developers in Nepal — anyone selling or renting flats, houses, land, or commercial space in Kathmandu, Pokhara, Lalitpur, Bhaktapur, Chitwan, or Butwal — who gets most of their enquiries from portals and Facebook and wants buyers to find them directly on Google.

## Who this is NOT for

This is not for a one-off seller with a single flat to sell this month. If you have one property and one deadline, list it on a portal and post it on Facebook today — SEO is the wrong tool for a one-time sale. This is also not a guide to gaming Google. There is no trick that puts a brand-new listings page above established portals overnight, and anyone promising that is selling you a fantasy.

## Buyers search first. Rank, or rent attention forever

When someone decides to buy a flat in Kathmandu, they do not start by calling an agent. They start by typing into Google. "2BHK flat for sale Kathmandu price." "Land for sale in Bhaktapur." "Apartment for rent in Pokhara." Those searches are the moment a buyer is ready — and the businesses that appear in them get the call.

Most Nepali real estate businesses never appear in those searches. Their website, if they have one, is a brochure: a homepage, an about page, and a contact form. Their listings live on portals, where they pay per listing or per lead, and where their properties sit next to ten competitors offering the same thing.

That is the core tradeoff of real estate in Nepal. **Portals rent you attention. Your own website builds an asset.** Every rupee spent on a portal listing disappears the day the listing expires. Every page on your own site that ranks keeps bringing enquiries, month after month, without another payment.

The answer is not to abandon portals. It is to stop depending on them. Use portals for reach, and use your own site for the searches that matter most — the ones where the buyer already knows what they want and where they want it.

## How property buyers actually search

Real estate search in Nepal falls into four patterns, and each one needs a different page on your site.

**Location + property type.** "Flats for sale in Kathmandu", "house for rent in Lalitpur", "land for sale in Pokhara". These are the highest-volume searches, and the hardest to win, because portals dominate them. You do not win these with one page. You win them with one good page per area — "Flats for sale in Baneshwor", "Flats for sale in Lazimpat" — each genuinely useful, with real listings, real prices, and real photos.

**Property type + price.** "2BHK flat Kathmandu price", "3BHK house for sale under 1 crore". Buyers who search with a price in mind are close to deciding. Pages that answer the price question honestly — with ranges, not "call for price" — earn the click and the call. Hiding prices is the single most common reason a listings page loses an enquiry.

**Area questions.** "Is Baneshwor a good place to buy a flat?" "Best area to live in Pokhara." Buyers research before they search listings. Short guides about each area — schools, hospitals, road access, water, what a typical flat costs — rank well, build trust, and send readers straight to your listings.

**Your brand and your listings.** "Your agency name", "flats by [developer name]". These should be the easiest searches to win, and yet many agencies lose them because their site has no page about their own projects. If someone searches your name and finds a portal instead of you, the portal gets the enquiry.

The pattern is the same one I describe in [keyword research for the Nepal market](/blog/keyword-research-nepal-english-vs-nepali-search): match the page to the search, in the language the searcher actually uses. Most Nepali property searches happen in English — "flats for sale Kathmandu" far outnumbers its Nepali-script equivalent — so write your listing and area pages in English, and add Nepali terms only where buyers genuinely use them.

## The pages a real estate site needs

A real estate website that ranks is not complicated. It needs five kinds of pages, and most Nepali agency sites have only one or two of them.

### 1. Location pages — one per area you serve

This is the highest-value work in real estate SEO. A page for "Flats for Sale in Baneshwor" should do what a buyer needs: current listings in Baneshwor with real prices and photos, a short honest description of the area, typical price ranges, and a way to enquire in one tap.

Do not build twenty empty location pages in a day. Build one good one, with real listings, then the next. A thin location page with no listings and copied text is worse than no page at all — Google ignores it, and a buyer who lands on it leaves immediately. The [homepage SEO](/blog/homepage-seo-nepal) rule applies here too: one page, one job, done properly.

### 2. Listing pages — one per property, built to be found

Every property you sell or rent deserves its own page, with its own URL, its own title tag, and its own photos. A listing buried inside a PDF or a Facebook post cannot rank. A listing page can.

What a good listing page contains:

- **A title tag that says what it is:** "2BHK Flat for Sale in Baneshwor, Kathmandu | NPR 1.25 Crore" — property type, area, city, price. That is what shows up in Google, and it is what the buyer clicks.
- **Real photos, compressed.** A listing with three blurry photos loses to a listing with twelve clear ones. Compress them — most of your buyers are on mobile data — and give each one a descriptive file name and alt text.
- **The facts buyers ask first:** price, area in square feet, bedrooms, bathrooms, facing, floor, parking, water supply, road access, and distance to the nearest school or hospital. Put them at the top, not at the bottom.
- **The price, in text.** Never "call for price" and never a price inside an image. Google cannot read a price from a photo, and buyers will not call to discover a number they could have read in five seconds.
- **A map and a way to enquire.** An embedded map, your phone number in text, and a WhatsApp button. In Nepal, WhatsApp is often the highest-converting call to action on a listing page.
- **RealEstateListing schema.** Structured data that tells Google this is a property listing, with its price, location, and features. I covered the basics in [schema markup for local businesses in Nepal](/blog/schema-markup-local-businesses-nepal) — the same principle applies, with property-specific fields.

Sold or rented listings should not be deleted. Mark them sold, keep the page live, and link to similar available properties. A deleted page loses its ranking and any links it earned; a "sold" page keeps working for you.

### 3. Area guides — the content that builds trust before the sale

A buyer choosing between Baneshwor and Kirtipur, or between Lakeside and Birauta, is not ready to call an agent yet. They are ready to read. Area guides — "Living in Baneshwor: prices, schools, and what to know before buying" — answer those questions, rank for searches your listings never will, and put your name in front of the buyer weeks before they enquire.

This is the same blogging strategy I laid out in [content that ranks](/blog/content-that-ranks-blogging-strategy-nepal): write the guide the buyer actually needs, not the article you wish they would read. One honest area guide a month beats ten generic "why invest in real estate" posts.

### 4. Your Google Business Profile — the free page that ranks first

For local searches — "real estate agent near me", "property dealer in Kathmandu" — your Google Business Profile often outranks your website. It is free, it shows in Maps, and it carries your reviews. Keep it complete: the right business name, your real address, your phone number, your hours, your services, and photos of your office and your properties.

Your profile and your website must tell the same story: same name, same address, same phone number. I covered the details in [local SEO for Kathmandu businesses](/blog/local-seo-kathmandu-google-business-profile), and everything there applies double to real estate, because your buyers are searching locally and deciding on trust.

### 5. Reviews — the proof that closes the deal

Nobody buys a flat from an agent they do not trust. Reviews are that trust, in public. Ask every happy buyer and seller for a Google review, and ask while the deal is fresh — the week the keys change hands, not six months later.

One genuine review with a name and a neighbourhood beats a paragraph of self-praise. And never invent reviews, ratings, or sales figures. A made-up "500+ happy families" line is easy to spot, and it costs you the one thing a property buyer will not give twice: trust.

## SEO vs portals vs Facebook: what each one actually does

| Channel | What it is good for | What it costs you | When to use it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Your own website (SEO) | Enquiries you own, that keep coming without paying per lead | Months of work before it pays; you must keep listings current | Always — this is the asset |
| Property portals | Reach today; buyers already search there | Per-listing fees; your listing sits next to competitors; you never own the traffic | For new listings and reach, while your site grows |
| Facebook / Instagram ads | Fast attention for one property or project | Stops the day you stop paying; enquiries are often low intent | For a launch, an open house, or a property that must move this month |
| Google Business Profile | Local and Maps searches, reviews, calls | Free, but needs regular photos and review replies | Always — it is free and it ranks |

The mistake is treating these as alternatives. They are layers. Portals and Facebook bring enquiries this month. Your site and your profile build the enquiries that arrive next year without another payment. An agency that only pays for attention is renting its pipeline. An agency that only does SEO starves while it waits. Do both, and shift the balance toward your own site as it grows.

## What real estate SEO costs, in time and money

There is no honest fixed price for real estate SEO in Nepal, and anyone who quotes one without seeing your site is guessing. What I can tell you honestly is where the time goes.

**The first month is foundations.** Fix the site structure, build the location pages for your top three areas, set up the Google Business Profile properly, and make sure every listing has its own page with a real price. This is the unglamorous work, and it is most of the value.

**Months two to six are content and listings.** One area guide a month, new listing pages as properties come in, reviews collected after every closed deal, and the slow accumulation of pages that Google starts to trust. This is also when most agencies quit — because the results are invisible at first. That is normal. I wrote about realistic timelines in [how long does SEO take](/blog/how-long-does-seo-take-nepal), and property is no exception: expect early signs in two to three months and real enquiry volume in six to twelve.

**Ongoing is maintenance, not reinvention.** Keep listings current, mark sold properties sold instead of deleting them, add a location page when you start working a new area, and reply to every review. A listings site that goes stale — sold flats still marked available, prices from last year — loses buyers and rankings together.

A monthly report should show enquiries and calls, not just rankings. If a report shows only "traffic is up" while your phone is quiet, the SEO is not working, no matter what the chart says. ## Common mistakes I see on Nepali property sites

**Listings with no price.** "Call for price" is the fastest way to lose a buyer to the next listing. Put the price in text, on the page.

**One page for all properties.** A single "Our Properties" page cannot rank for "flats in Baneshwor" and "land in Pokhara" at the same time. One property, one page.

**Photos as the whole listing.** A listing that is one big image with the details burned into it looks fine and ranks for nothing. Google reads text. Put the facts in text.

**Deleting sold listings.** Every deleted page is a ranking thrown away. Mark it sold, keep it live, link to similar properties.

**Copying portal descriptions.** If your listing page says exactly what the portal says, Google has no reason to show yours. Write your own description, with details only someone who visited the property would know — the light in the afternoon, the actual road width, the water situation in dry season.

**A homepage that tries to sell everything.** Your homepage should rank for your brand and your main service plus city. The location and listing pages do the rest. I covered this division of labour in [homepage SEO](/blog/homepage-seo-nepal).

**Ignoring mobile speed.** Most property buyers browse on their phone, on mobile data, often while standing outside the property. A listing page that takes ten seconds to load loses the buyer before the photos appear. Compress images, drop the auto-playing video, and test every page on a mid-range phone. The details are in [Core Web Vitals and site speed for Nepal's mobile users](/blog/core-web-vitals-site-speed-nepal-mobile).

## FAQs

### How long does real estate SEO take in Nepal?

Expect early signs — a location page appearing for its area, more calls from Google Maps — in two to three months, and meaningful enquiry volume in six to twelve months. Property SEO is slower than most niches because portals dominate the biggest keywords, so your first wins come from specific area and price searches, not from "flats for sale in Kathmandu". If someone promises page one in thirty days, they are either targeting a keyword nobody searches or planning to take your money and disappear.

### Should I still list on property portals if I do SEO?

Yes — at least while your own site is growing. Portals have the buyers today, and a new listing there can bring enquiries this week. The goal is not to replace portals overnight. It is to build your own site until the day you can choose which portal listings are worth paying for, instead of depending on them for every enquiry. List on portals, but always link back to your own listing page where the portal allows it, and always keep your own page as the complete version.

### What is the most important page on a real estate website?

Your location pages, not your homepage. A buyer searching "flats for sale in Baneshwor" will never land on your homepage — they will land on the page about Baneshwor, or on a competitor's. Build one genuinely useful page per area you serve, with real listings, real prices, and honest area information, before you spend anything on design.

### How many listings do I need before SEO works?

You do not need hundreds. Ten good listing pages with real prices and photos, plus two or three location pages, is enough to start. What matters is that every listing is a real, complete page — not a thumbnail on a grid. A small agency with twenty excellent pages will outrank a large one with two hundred thin ones, because Google rewards pages that answer the search, not pages that exist.

### Do I need a blog for real estate SEO?

You need area guides, which are a blog by another name. One honest guide a month — a neighbourhood, a price trend you can document, a step-by-step for first-time buyers — does more than a weekly stream of generic property news. Buyers read guides when they are deciding where to buy, and Google ranks the guide that answers the question best. Skip the generic posts. Write what only someone who sells in that area would know.

### Should my listings be in English or Nepali?

English, for the pages themselves. Most Nepali property searches happen in English — "flat for sale Kathmandu", "land for sale Pokhara" — and your buyers, including the diaspora buying from abroad, search in English too. Add Nepali terms where buyers genuinely use them, such as area names people search in Devanagari, but do not translate the whole site. Match the language of the search, not the language of the office.

### How do I get Google reviews as a real estate agent?

Ask at the moment the deal closes, when the buyer is happiest. Send a short message with a direct link to your Google Business Profile review page — one tap, no searching. Make it easy and specific: "It would help us a lot if you shared your experience — it takes a minute." Then reply to every review, good or bad, within a day. A replied-to review tells the next buyer you are a real person who stands behind your work.

### Can SEO work for a small agency competing with big portals?

Yes, but not by fighting portals for the biggest keywords. You win the searches portals serve badly: a specific area, a specific price range, a specific property type, a question about a neighbourhood. Portals are built for breadth. You are built for depth — you know the street, the building, the owner. A location page with real local knowledge beats a portal page with a thousand listings and no soul. That is the whole strategy: be the most useful result for the searches you can win, and let the portals have the ones you cannot.

## The bottom line

Property buyers in Nepal start on Google, and they call the business they find there. Portals will rent you that attention for as long as you pay. Your own site — one page per property, one page per area, prices in text, photos that load fast, reviews that prove you close — builds attention you own.

Then do the next area. SEO for real estate is not a campaign with an end date. It is the habit of being findable, and it compounds.
