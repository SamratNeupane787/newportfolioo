## TL;DR

- Your homepage is usually your most powerful page — it carries the most authority, gets the most links, and is the first thing most visitors and Google see.
- Most Nepali business homepages waste it: a vague hero line, a slider nobody reads, and no clear next step.
- Fix it with one clear value proposition, your main service or keyword in the H1, proof that builds trust, and one obvious call to action.
- A homepage that ranks and converts is a decision, not a design trend. Treat it like your best salesperson, not a brochure.

## Who this is for

This guide is for Nepali business owners, clinic and hotel managers, and freelancers whose website homepage gets traffic but produces few enquiries. If your homepage is the first page people land on — and for most small sites it is — this is for you.

## Who this is NOT for

This is not for sites where the homepage is a deliberate gateway to a logged-in app or dashboard. If your homepage's job is to get existing users inside, different rules apply. This is also not a guide to gaming Google with keyword stuffing — the tactics here are slow, honest, and built to last.

## Your homepage has one job

Most homepages try to do everything. They introduce the company, list every service, show a slideshow, feature a news item from two years ago, and link to the Facebook page. The result is a page that does nothing well.

Your homepage has one job: **convince the right visitor, in about five seconds, that they are in the right place — and show them what to do next.**

Google rewards the same thing visitors reward. A homepage that clearly says what the business does, who it serves, and where, and that answers the visitor's question quickly, is easier for Google to understand and rank. A homepage that confuses visitors is a homepage that confuses Google too.

Think of your homepage as your best salesperson. Your best salesperson does not recite the company history to every person who walks in. They ask what the person needs, point them to the right thing, and make it easy to say yes.

## What a homepage should actually contain

A high-performing homepage for a Nepali business is not complicated. It needs seven things, in roughly this order.

### 1. One clear headline that says what you do

The first line a visitor reads should answer three questions: what do you do, who is it for, and where are you?

"We provide quality services" says none of those things. "Dental clinic in Kathmandu — same-day appointments, transparent pricing" says all three.

Your H1 should carry your most important keyword naturally. If you are a clinic in Pokhara, your H1 can be something like "Dental Care in Pokhara — Gentle Treatment, Clear Prices." That is a headline a human understands and a keyword Google can rank.

### 2. One sentence of proof

Right under the headline, give the visitor a reason to believe you. Proof is specific. "Trusted by hundreds of patients in Kathmandu" is vague. "4.9 stars from 200+ Google reviews" is proof — if it is true. Never invent a number. Use real reviews, real years in business, real results.

My own proof is measured, not claimed: I grew a client ecommerce site from near-zero search visibility to **265K Google Search impressions and 6.76K clicks in 8 months**, with an average position of 8.2. That is the kind of number a homepage should carry — one you can show, not one you made up.

### 3. Your core services, in plain language

List your three to five main services, each with one line explaining who it is for and what it solves. Link each one to its own service page. This is where your homepage starts working for SEO: those internal links pass authority from your strongest page to the pages that need to rank for specific services.

A hotel homepage might list rooms, dining, and trekking packages. A clinic might list general checkups, dental care, and diagnostics. Keep it to what you actually sell, not everything you could sell.

### 4. Why choose you — three reasons, not ten

Pick the three things that genuinely set you apart and say them plainly. "We answer the phone." "Same-day appointments." "Prices on the website, no surprises." These are small claims, but they are the ones that move a visitor from browsing to booking.

This is also where most Nepali business homepages go wrong: they list ten generic qualities — quality, trust, excellence, commitment — and none of them mean anything. Three honest reasons beat ten empty ones.

### 5. Real proof from real people

Reviews, short testimonials, before-and-after stories, logos of clients or partners you actually worked with. One genuine Google review with a name and a place beats a paragraph of self-praise.

If you have no reviews yet, that is your first job — not a redesign. Ask your last five happy customers for a Google review. A homepage with zero reviews and a beautiful design still loses to a plain homepage with twenty real reviews.

### 6. One clear call to action, repeated

Every section of your homepage should end with the same next step: book, call, message on WhatsApp, or get a quote. Repeat it. Visitors do not scroll back up to find your phone number.

In Nepal, WhatsApp is often the highest-converting call to action. A "Chat on WhatsApp" button that opens a conversation is worth more than a contact form nobody fills in. Put your phone number in text, not inside an image, so Google and visitors can read it.

### 7. The local details Google needs

Your city, your area, your neighbourhood. "Kathmandu" is not enough if your customers search "dentist in Baneshwor" or "hotel near Lakeside Pokhara." Name the area you serve, naturally, in the copy — and put your full address and opening hours in the footer.

This is the foundation of [local SEO for Kathmandu businesses](/blog/local-seo-kathmandu-google-business-profile). Your homepage and your Google Business Profile should tell the same story: same name, same address, same phone number, same services.

## The homepage SEO checklist

Use this as a working checklist. If an item is missing, it is a fix worth making this week.

| Element | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| Title tag | Business name + main service + city, under 60 characters |
| H1 | One H1, your main keyword, in plain language |
| Meta description | One clear sentence with the service, the city, and a reason to click |
| Hero headline | What you do, who it is for, where you are — in one line |
| First 100 words | The answer to the visitor's question, not company history |
| Internal links | Every core service linked to its own page with descriptive anchor text |
| Images | Real photos, compressed, with descriptive alt text |
| Speed | Loads fast on a mid-range phone on Nepali mobile data |
| Call to action | One clear next step, repeated, visible without scrolling |
| NAP | Name, address, phone — identical to your Google Business Profile |
| Schema | LocalBusiness schema with name, address, phone, hours, and services |

Two of these deserve more attention, because they are the ones I see missed most often in Nepal.

### Your title tag is your search result

The title tag is what shows up as the blue link in Google. Most Nepali homepages have a title tag that just says the business name — "ABC Dental" — or worse, "Home." That is a wasted ranking opportunity.

A good homepage title tag is: **Service + City | Business Name**. "Dental Clinic in Kathmandu | ABC Dental Care" tells Google and the searcher exactly what the page is. Keep it under 60 characters so it does not get cut off.

### Speed is a ranking factor and a conversion factor

Most of your visitors are on mobile, on data that is slower and more expensive than yours. A homepage with a 5 MB slideshow, three pop-ups, and a chat widget that loads before the content will lose visitors before they read a word — and Google notices.

Compress your images, drop the auto-playing slider, and load the chat widget after the page content. I covered the details in [Core Web Vitals and site speed for Nepal's mobile users](/blog/core-web-vitals-site-speed-nepal-mobile).

## Common homepage mistakes I see in Nepal

**The slider.** A rotating banner of five messages means visitors read none of them. Pick the single most important message and show it. Sliders also slow the page down and push your real content below the fold.

**The "Welcome to our website" headline.** Nobody searches for "welcome." Your headline is your most valuable on-page SEO real estate. Spend it on what you do and where you do it.

**Hiding prices.** "Call for price" is not a strategy, it is a leak. Visitors who cannot see a price range leave and ask the next business. You do not need a full price list — a starting price or a typical range is enough to keep them.

**No contact details above the fold.** Your phone number and WhatsApp button should be visible the moment the page loads. If a visitor has to hunt for how to reach you, you have already lost them.

**Copying a competitor's homepage.** If your homepage reads like every other clinic, hotel, or agency in Kathmandu, Google has no reason to rank you above them — and neither does a visitor. Say what is actually different about you, in your own words.

**Keyword stuffing.** Repeating "best dentist Kathmandu best dentist Kathmandu" in your footer does not help you rank. It makes your site look spammy, and Google is very good at spotting it now. Write for people first, and put the keyword where it belongs: the title tag, the H1, and naturally in the copy.

## Homepage vs landing page: know the difference

Your homepage serves everyone — every service, every kind of visitor. A landing page serves one person with one need: someone searching "root canal cost Kathmandu" should land on a page about root canals, not your homepage.

So do not try to make your homepage rank for everything. Let it rank for your brand name and your main service + city. Build separate pages for the specific services and questions, and link to them from the homepage. That is how small sites grow: the homepage is the hub, and the service pages are the spokes.

## FAQs

### How long should a homepage be?

Long enough to answer the visitor's questions, short enough that they reach the call to action. For most Nepali businesses, that is a page that takes about one to two minutes to scan: headline, proof, services, reasons to choose you, reviews, and a clear next step. A homepage that is only a hero image and a footer is too short. A homepage that scrolls for five minutes before the phone number appears is too long.

### Should my homepage target one keyword or many?

One primary keyword — your main service plus your city. A dental clinic in Kathmandu targets "dental clinic in Kathmandu" on the homepage. Everything else — teeth whitening, root canal, braces — gets its own page, linked from the homepage. Trying to rank a homepage for ten keywords usually means it ranks for none of them.

### How often should I update my homepage?

Review it every three to six months. Update the reviews, refresh the offers, and check that the phone number, hours, and prices are still right. You do not need to redesign it every year. A homepage that is kept accurate beats a homepage that is redesigned constantly and never finished.

### Does my homepage need a blog section?

It helps. A short "latest from the blog" section with two or three recent posts gives visitors a reason to stay, gives Google fresh content on your most important page, and links your homepage's authority to your articles. Keep it to headlines and dates — the articles do the talking.

### What is the single most important homepage SEO fix?

Your title tag and H1. If they say nothing about what you do and where you do it, fix those first — they take ten minutes and they change what Google shows for your site. After that, fix your call to action, because ranking without converting is just traffic.

### Can a homepage rank without backlinks?

For a brand name and a low-competition local keyword, often yes — a clear, fast, well-structured homepage with a strong Google Business Profile can rank locally without many backlinks. For competitive keywords, backlinks still matter. But no amount of backlinks will save a homepage that confuses visitors. Fix the page first, then build links to it.

### Should the homepage be in English or Nepali?

Match your customers. Most Nepali businesses serve customers who search in English — "dentist in Kathmandu" far outnumbers its Nepali-script equivalent in search volume. Write the homepage in English, and add Nepali where it genuinely helps a visitor, such as a local term your customers actually use. I wrote about this in detail in [keyword research for the Nepal market](/blog/keyword-research-nepal-english-vs-nepali-search).

### How do I know if my homepage is working?

Watch three numbers: how many people arrive on it from Google, how many click your call to action, and how many of those become enquiries. If traffic is growing but enquiries are flat, the problem is the page, not the SEO. If enquiries are growing, leave the homepage alone and fix the next page.

## The bottom line

Your homepage is the most powerful page on your site, and for most Nepali businesses it is the most wasted. It does not need a redesign. It needs a decision: one clear headline, one real proof, your core services, three honest reasons to choose you, and one obvious next step.

Make that decision this week. Fix the title tag and the H1 today, add your real reviews tomorrow, and put the phone number where nobody has to look for it. Then measure what happens. A homepage that ranks and converts is not a design project — it is a salesperson that works while you sleep.
