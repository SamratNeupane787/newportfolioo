When someone in Kathmandu wakes up with a toothache, a skin problem, or a parent who needs a check-up, what do they do first? They search Google. "Dentist near me." "Best dermatologist in Kathmandu." "Physiotherapy clinic Lalitpur." If your clinic does not show up in those results, those patients are booking with the clinic that does — even if you are the better doctor.

This guide is for clinic owners, hospital marketing teams, and individual practitioners in Nepal who want patients to find them through Google search. It is **not** for anyone looking for a quick trick to outrank competitors overnight, and it is **not** a guide to running Facebook ads — that is a different channel with different rules.

**TL;DR**
- Patients search Google before choosing a clinic, and most never scroll past the first few results.
- For clinics, local SEO matters more than anything else: your Google Business Profile, your reviews, and your location pages decide whether you appear in the map results.
- Health content is held to a higher standard by Google — every claim must be accurate, and the doctor behind the advice must be visible.
- A clinic website needs service pages for every treatment, a doctor profile for every practitioner, and a booking path that works on a phone in under a minute.
- SEO for a clinic is a 3–6 month investment, not a one-time fix. The clinics that win are the ones that keep publishing, keep collecting reviews, and keep their information accurate.

## Who this is for

- **Private clinics in Kathmandu, Lalitpur, and Pokhara** — dental, dermatology, eye care, physiotherapy, diagnostics, and general practice clinics competing for local patients.
- **Hospitals and multi-speciality centres** that want their departments and doctors to be found individually, not just under the hospital name.

## Why patients search before they choose

A patient choosing a clinic is making a high-trust decision. Unlike choosing a restaurant, the cost of a wrong choice feels real. So patients research:

1. They search the condition or treatment — "root canal cost Kathmandu" or "skin allergy treatment Nepal."
2. They compare the clinics that appear — reading reviews, checking photos, looking at the doctor's qualifications.
3. They check the practical details — location, opening hours, phone number, whether appointments are available today.
4. Then they call, message on WhatsApp, or walk in.

Every step in that journey is a search result, a listing, or a web page. If your clinic is invisible at step one, steps two to four never happen.

This is the same behaviour I have written about for [local businesses in Kathmandu](/blog/local-seo-kathmandu-google-business-profile) — but for clinics, the trust bar is higher and the stakes are higher.

## Start with your Google Business Profile

For a clinic, the Google Business Profile is more important than the website. When someone searches "dentist near me," Google shows a map with three clinics before it shows any website links. Your profile decides whether you are one of those three.

Get these right:

- **Business name:** your real clinic name, exactly as it appears on your signboard. Do not stuff keywords into the name — Google removes listings for that.
- **Category:** pick the most accurate primary category ("Dental Clinic," "Dermatologist," "Physiotherapy Centre"), then add relevant secondary categories.
- **Services:** list every service you offer, with a short description. This is how you appear for treatment searches.
- **Hours:** keep them accurate, including public holidays. Nothing loses a patient faster than arriving at a closed clinic.
- **Photos:** real photos of the reception, treatment rooms, equipment, and the team. Patients want to see where they are going.
- **Posts:** use the profile's post feature for health camps, new services, and seasonal advice — it signals that the clinic is active.
- **Q&A:** answer the questions patients actually ask — "Do you take walk-ins?" "What is the consultation fee?" — before someone else answers them for you.

## Reviews are the currency of clinic SEO

Reviews do three things at once for a clinic: they influence the map ranking, they convince patients who are comparing you with the clinic next door, and they surface in your profile as the first thing a patient reads.

- **Ask every satisfied patient.** A simple line at the reception desk or a WhatsApp message after the visit is enough: "If you were happy with your visit, a Google review helps other patients find us."
- **Never buy reviews and never fake them.** Google removes fake reviews, patients can tell, and one exposed fake review damages trust more than ten real ones build it.
- **Reply to every review, including the bad ones.** A calm, professional reply to a complaint — "We're sorry about the wait, and we have changed our scheduling" — tells future patients more about your clinic than a hundred five-star reviews.
- **Do not respond to medical details in public.** If a review mentions a treatment outcome, keep the reply general and invite the patient to contact the clinic directly.

## One page per service, one page per doctor

A clinic website that lists all its services on one page ranks for almost nothing. Google ranks pages, not websites. A patient searching "dental implants Kathmandu" should land on a page about dental implants in Kathmandu — not your homepage.

Build a page for every service you genuinely offer:

- What the treatment is, in plain language a patient understands.
- Who it is for and who it is not for.
- What happens during the visit, step by step.
- How long it takes and how many visits are usually needed.
- What it costs, or at least the consultation fee and the factors that change the price. Clinics that hide prices lose patients to clinics that state them.
- Which doctor performs it, with a link to their profile.

And give every doctor a profile page: full name, qualifications, years of experience, specialities, languages spoken, and the days they are available. Patients search doctors by name — "Dr [name] Kathmandu" — and a real profile page is what should rank, not a social media post.

## Health content: accurate, attributed, and honest

Google treats health content as "Your Money or Your Life" content — pages that can affect a person's health are held to the highest standard. That means:

- **Every article must be reviewed by a qualified doctor.** Put the reviewer's name and credentials on the page. Anonymous health advice does not rank and should not.
- **Write for patients, not for doctors.** A patient searching "why does my tooth hurt when I drink cold water" wants a clear answer in plain language, not a textbook paragraph.
- **Answer the question directly, then explain.** Start with the short answer, then give the detail, then say when to see a doctor. This is the same answer-first structure I use on this blog.
- **Never promise outcomes.** "Treatment can help" is honest. "Guaranteed pain-free in one visit" is not — and patients remember the difference.
- **Cite authoritative sources** when you state a fact about a condition or treatment — the World Health Organization, Nepal's Ministry of Health and Population, or peer-reviewed guidance — and link to them.

Good topics for clinic content: the questions patients already ask at reception. "How often should I get my eyes checked?" "Is a root canal painful?" "When should a child first see a dentist?" Each question is a search, and each honest answer is a page that can bring patients in for years.

## The website itself: fast, mobile, and bookable

Most patients in Nepal search on a phone, often on a slow connection. A clinic website that takes ten seconds to load loses the patient before the first sentence. This is the same mobile-first reality I wrote about in [Core Web Vitals and Site Speed for Nepal's Mobile Users](/blog/core-web-vitals-site-speed-nepal-mobile) — and for clinics it matters more, because the patient is often in pain or in a hurry.

Check these basics:

- **Loads in under three seconds on a phone.** Compress images, remove what you do not need, and test on a real phone, not only on office wifi.
- **Phone number and WhatsApp button visible on every page.** The patient should be able to call or message in one tap, without hunting for a contact page.
- **Online booking or appointment request that takes under a minute.** Every extra field loses patients. Name, phone, service, preferred time — that is enough to start.
- **Location and directions on every page.** A map embed, the neighbourhood, and nearby landmarks. "Opposite the Bhatbhateni, Pulchowk" helps a patient more than a street address alone.
- **HTTPS and a working contact form.** Patients share health details; a secure site is not optional.

## Schema markup for clinics

Schema markup is structured data that tells Google exactly what your pages are about. For a clinic, the most useful types are:

- **LocalBusiness / MedicalBusiness** on the homepage and contact page, with name, address, phone, hours, and geo-coordinates.
- **Physician** on each doctor profile, with name, specialty, and qualifications.
- **MedicalProcedure** on service pages, with the procedure name and a plain-language description.
- **FAQPage** on pages that answer common questions.

Schema does not guarantee a ranking boost, but it helps Google understand your clinic and show richer results — review stars, hours, and services directly in search. ## FAQs

**How long does SEO take for a clinic in Nepal?**
For a clinic starting from scratch, expect 3–6 months before search brings a steady stream of booked appointments. The Google Business Profile and reviews can improve map visibility within weeks, but service pages and health content need time to be indexed, tested by Google, and trusted. Clinics in less competitive specialities or smaller cities may see results sooner; a dental clinic in central Kathmandu faces more competition and needs more time.

**Do small clinics need a website, or is a Google Business Profile enough?**
A profile alone can bring calls, and it should be your first priority. But a website is what lets you rank for treatment searches ("root canal Kathmandu"), publish doctor profiles, and answer patient questions in depth. A profile gets you found on the map; a website gets you found for everything else. Small clinics should start with the profile, then add the website's service pages one at a time.

**How do clinics get more Google reviews without breaking the rules?**
Ask satisfied patients directly — at reception, by SMS, or by WhatsApp after the visit — and make it easy with a direct review link. You may not pay for reviews, offer discounts in exchange for them, or write them yourself. You may ask, and you may reply to every review you receive. A steady flow of genuine reviews beats a sudden burst every time.

**What should a clinic never do for SEO?**
Never buy backlinks or reviews, never stuff keywords into your business name, never copy content from another clinic's website, and never publish health advice that no doctor has reviewed. Google penalises all of these, and in health, patients penalise them harder. If an agency promises first-page rankings in a month for a clinic, that is a promise they cannot honestly keep.

**Is SEO worth it for a single-doctor practice?**
Yes, often more so than for a large hospital. A single doctor's name, speciality, and location are specific searches with clear intent — "skin doctor in Pokhara" — and a well-built profile plus a doctor page can win them. The cost is lower because there are fewer services and fewer pages to build, and the return is direct: every new patient who finds you through search is a patient you did not pay an ad platform to reach.

**Should a clinic run Google Ads as well as SEO?**
For a new clinic that needs patients immediately, yes — ads bring patients while SEO builds. Over time, the balance should shift: SEO compounds, while ads stop the moment you stop paying. A practical approach is to run ads for your highest-value treatments and build SEO for the same services, so that when the SEO pages rank, you can reduce the ad spend on those treatments.

**What is the most common SEO mistake clinics make?**
Listing every service on a single page with no detail, and having no doctor profiles at all. Patients search for specific treatments and specific doctors, and Google ranks specific pages. A clinic with ten thin pages — one per service, one per doctor — will outrank a clinic with one long page that tries to say everything at once.

**How much should a clinic in Nepal budget for SEO?**
It depends on the number of services, the number of doctors, and how competitive the speciality is in your city. What matters more than the monthly figure is what the work includes: profile management, review process, service and doctor pages, content reviewed by a clinician, and reporting on booked appointments rather than vanity metrics.
